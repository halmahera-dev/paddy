# West Java rice farmers: field problems with rotation, water, and soil

**Checked:** 19 October 2026. **Purpose:** give the Field Shift problem statement a base of secondary evidence. The team cannot speak to farmers or extension workers. This note does not repeat the national data in [indonesia-agriculture-and-data.md](indonesia-agriculture-and-data.md).

**Labels.** **Fact** = a source states it; the link follows. **Inference** = our reading of the facts. Many good numbers are old (2012–2019). We give the year each time.

## Decision

1. **Change the PRD problem statement.** The sources do not support "Java farmers often plant rice in every season". In West Java, the usual irrigated pattern is two rice crops and then palawija or fallow. Dry-season rice (MK1, *gadu*) is normal and often pays well. The documented losses come from a narrower case. A farmer plants a late or **third rice crop (MK2, *gadu II*, MT3)**. The field is at the **tail end of a canal**, or it is rainfed land on pumps. Then a dry year (often El Niño) cuts the water.
2. **The farmer does not decide alone.** The irrigation operator and the water schedule set *when* water comes. The person who works the field chooses *what* to plant inside that limit. Often this person is a tenant, not the owner. Government programs push farmers to plant *more* rice per year.
3. **The soil problem is real but weakly sourced for West Java.** We found national claims and one Karawang thesis. Use soil as a second reason, not the lead problem.

### Proposed problem statement (inference)

> In West Java's main rice districts, many farmers plant a third or late dry-season rice crop when the water supply is uncertain. Fields at the tail end of canals, or on rainfed land that depends on pumps, fail first in dry years. In 2015, 21,050 ha failed in Indramayu alone. Farmers, water groups, and district staff have no simple way to see, before they plant, whether a field's water and rain will support rice. They also cannot easily compare it with maize, soybean, mung bean, or a rest period.

| Rainfed (*tadah hujan*) | Rice–palawija or rice–fallow (IP100). The government now adds a second rice crop with pumps. | Sumedang (July 2026): Jatinunggal areas with IP1 "we direct to other crops". **Area shares.** BPS 2014 data, as cited by BPTP Jabar, gives 942,974 ha of West Java sawah. About **40%** has technical, semi-technical, or simple irrigation. About **60% (565,784 ha)** has village irrigation, no irrigation, or depends on rain. [Kementan repository](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/bitstreams/ec16fe9b-1bee-4be2-b5d5-ca28f11aacca/download) (fact, 2014). The 2026 base sawah area is **900,772 ha**. [Republika, 29 Mar 2026](https://ekonomi.republika.co.id/berita/tcnirj423/kementan-jaga-produksi-padi-jabar-hadapi-ancaman-kemarau-2026). Karawang has about **87,000 ha** of technical irrigated sawah. [Antara, 15 Aug 2026](https://megapolitan.antaranews.com/berita/545189/bupati-karawang-koordinasikan-dampak-kemarau-terhadap-sektor-pertanian). We found **no** current BPS table of sawah area by irrigation type for each kabupaten. This is a gap.

**Inference:** "Dry season" has two parts. MK1 (*gadu*, about Mar–Jun) rice on irrigated land is normal and not the problem. MK2 / *gadu II* / MT3 (about Jul–Oct) is the real choice between rice, palawija, and fallow. Pilot advice must know the irrigation type and the canal position, not only the rain.

[BSIP Kalteng](https://kalteng.bsip.pertanian.go.id/berita/percepatan-pompanisasi-wamentan-sudaryono-pemerintah-komitmen-kebijakan-pro-petani). | District Dinas advice | It asks farmers to plant earlier or to change crops. These are requests, not orders. | Majalengka 2026: plant MT2 earlier. Sumedang 2026: grow palawija in IP1 areas. Indramayu 2012: letters to drought-prone kecamatan to change to palawija. Farmers often ignore this advice. [TIMES Indonesia, 13 Jul 2026](https://timesindonesia.co.id/peristiwa/598867/ancaman-di-balik-kemarau-ribuan-hektare-sawah-di-majalengka-dihantui-kekeringan) · [Metro TV 2026](https://www.metrotvnews.com/read/NG9Czoqn-petani-sumedang-diminta-beralih-ke-tanaman-palawija-selama-kemarau) · [Republika, 28 Apr 2012](https://news.republika.co.id/berita/m370fz/musim-kemarau-petani-nekat-tanam-padi) (fact) |
| Owner vs. tenant | In *sakap* (sharecropping), the owner gives the land and the tenant gives the labour. In *sewa* (rent), the tenant takes all the risk. | Search excerpts from Indonesian tenure studies (Ciparay, Bandung case); the source was not opened. [Bisnis, 4 Dec 2023](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20231204/9/1720611/miris-bps-catat-jumlah-petani-gurem-naik-jadi-1689-juta). We found **no** West Java study on who chooses the crop when the owner and the tenant are different people. (fact; gap) |

**Inference (who really decides):** The person who works the field chooses the crop. This can be the owner or the tenant. The irrigation schedule and the canal position limit that choice. Government IP targets and pumps push the choice toward more rice. A landowner who rents the land out may not decide the crop at all. So the PRD main user ("the farmer or landowner who decides what is planted") is only partly correct. The most realistic users with leverage on *where* rice is risky are the **water-management group (P3A/GP3A), the PPL, and the district Dinas**. They decide where and when water and pumps go.

**Gaps:** We found no final West Java puso total for 2019, 2023, or 2026. The reports use different dates and categories (threatened, light, puso). Do not add them together. Ask Distan Jabar or the Kementan crop-damage reports (OPT/DPI) for official totals.

**Inference:** The pattern repeats across years and places. Puso concentrates (a) at the tail ends of canals, (b) in late dry-season plantings that need pumps, and (c) in El Niño years. Canal silt and illegal pumping also cut the water at the tail end. This is a better problem than "rain is too low". Rain is one input. Canal delivery is the input that usually fails.

**Inference:** The low organic carbon and the dependence on fertiliser have good support at national level. We found no West Java time series that proves a levelling-off of productivity caused by continuous rice. Do not make a claim that the field results cannot support. A safe wording is: "National soil agencies report low organic matter in most irrigated sawah. A legume or rest period is a standard way to add residue and break pest cycles." Local rules (RTT) already name soil fertility and pest cycles as reasons for crop rotation.

## Three strongest problem candidates

1. **Late or third rice crop fails at canal tail ends in dry years.** Repeated, numeric, and local evidence: Indramayu 21,050 ha (2015) and about 7,500 ha (2019); Karawang about 1,000 ha (2023, 2026); Majalengka MT3 stopped (2026). Each report names the tail end of the canal.
2. **Farmers lack a pre-season signal to choose rice, palawija, or a rest period.** Farmers say they "gamble" on rice. The Boer & Surmaini model shows a 19–45% income gain from changing crops with early climate information. Dinas requests to change crops are ignored without evidence for each area.
3. **Programs push more rice crops per year without a water and soil check.** Pumps for 201,542 ha of rainfed land and IP300 targets are planned. National agencies report low organic matter in most irrigated sawah. West Java soil evidence is thin.

**Product inference.** A NASA-based tool fits candidate 1 and candidate 2 best. Show, for each kecamatan or irrigation block, the IMERG rain and SMAP moisture in past MK2 seasons, especially El Niño years such as 2015, 2019, and 2023. Then compare a third rice crop with maize, mung bean, soybean, or fallow. Label canal position and irrigation type as local inputs that NASA cannot see.
